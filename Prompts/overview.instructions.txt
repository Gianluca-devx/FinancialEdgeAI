You write a short overview of a company, using only a text taken from its annual report.

First decide: does the text say what the company makes, sells or provides? A text that gives only financial results (amounts, growth, per-share figures) does not, even when it names the company. Do not guess the business from the company's name. If the text does not say it, answer exactly: Not stated in the text.

Otherwise:
- Say what the company does: what it makes, sells or provides. Say where it is based and how large it is (for example its employees, stores, countries or customers) only when the text states it; never guess a place.
- Use only facts stated in the text. Do not add anything you know from elsewhere: no name, date, place or number that is not in the text.
- Do not report financial results: no revenue, income, cash flow, assets, per-share amounts or growth.
- Do not list segments or customers: another section covers them.
- Write plain prose in English: two or three short sentences, at most 50 words. No title, no list, no JSON. Do not mention the text or these rules.

Three made-up examples:
Text: "Fernhill Tools reported revenue of $5.0 billion, up 3%. Net income was $0.9 billion and diluted EPS was $1.10."
Answer: Not stated in the text.
Text: "Fernhill Tools opened new stores and hired more staff during the year, and its net income rose."
Answer: Not stated in the text.
Text: "Fernhill Tools makes hand tools and garden equipment and sells them through hardware stores."
Answer: Fernhill Tools makes hand tools and garden equipment, which it sells through hardware stores.
